## Pull the facts before comparing years

A December-versus-January decision changes more than the payment date. The two sales can fall in different tax years, use different inflation-adjusted brackets, stack on different amounts of income, and trigger different state residency or source rules.

Gather the expected sale price, adjusted basis, selling costs, acquisition date, filing status, projected taxable income for each year, realized gains and losses, capital-loss carryforwards, and any planned move. Record the assumptions separately for December and January.

Do not assume the sale date is the day cash reaches a bank account. The tax date can depend on trade settlement, closing terms, constructive receipt, or a binding contract. Confirm the applicable rule for the asset.

## Check the holding period first

Waiting a few weeks can move a sale from short-term to long-term treatment when the asset crosses the more-than-one-year threshold. That potential difference is often larger than a small inflation adjustment between annual brackets.

If both dates are already long-term, compare how much room remains in the 0% and 15% brackets after other taxable income is considered. Preferential gain stacks on top of that income; it does not receive a fresh bracket independent of wages, business income, and other taxable items.

The 2026 scenario planner can compare two sale dates inside 2026. It cannot apply a 2027 table or calculate a blended two-year result. A date adjustment outside 2026 is capped to the first or last day of the supported year and displayed in the result.

## Build a two-column worksheet

Create a December column and a January column with these rows:

1. Expected gross proceeds.
2. Adjusted basis and transaction costs.
3. Short-term and long-term gains or losses.
4. Other projected taxable income.
5. Federal capital gains estimate using that year’s official table.
6. NIIT calculated separately from MAGI and net investment income.
7. State and local tax using the correct residency and source rules.
8. Required estimated payment and its due date.
9. Market, financing, and carrying costs of waiting.
10. Net after-tax cash under the same risk assumptions.

This structure prevents a lower displayed capital gains number from hiding a bonus, business sale, required distribution, loss carryforward, or state event that changes the total.

## Payment timing is not the same as tax savings

A January sale can defer when tax is reported, but it does not create a permanent saving by itself. Estimated-payment rules may require cash before the annual return is filed. Underpayment safe harbors and annualized-income methods depend on the taxpayer’s facts and prior-year liability.

Likewise, a December sale does not always mean an immediate penalty. Withholding is generally treated differently from quarterly estimated payments, and the annualized-income method may reflect when income was actually earned. Use current Form 1040-ES and Form 2210 instructions for a real payment calculation.

## Include taxes the site does not calculate

Gain Tax Calculator excludes NIIT, alternative minimum tax, additional Medicare tax, local income tax, special collectibles rates, qualified small business stock, installment-sale rules, and many state adjustments.

Employee stock options require special caution. An ISO exercise and later sale can involve compensation income, alternative minimum tax, and two holding-period tests. The site scenario planner is not an option calculator and should not be used to choose an exercise strategy.

Real estate can also cross years through installment payments or delayed closings. Depreciation recapture, section 121 exclusions, withholding, and source rules need transaction-specific review.

## Consider non-tax risks

Waiting changes exposure to market price, interest, property carrying costs, counterparty risk, and personal liquidity. A projected tax benefit can disappear after a modest price move.

State the break-even amount: how far can the price move before waiting costs more than the estimated tax difference? Use conservative values and consider whether a partial sale can meet liquidity or diversification goals without making an all-or-nothing timing bet.

## Final review

- Use official tables for each tax year rather than reusing 2026 rates.
- Keep federal, NIIT, state, and local calculations separate.
- Confirm the legal sale date and holding period.
- Compare after-tax cash with market and carrying costs.
- Document the assumptions and review a material decision before acting.

The scenario planner is useful for a preliminary comparison inside its stated 2026 scope. A cross-year decision requires two supported tax-year calculations and a broader cash-flow analysis.
