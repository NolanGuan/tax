## Start with realized results, not the color of a position

An unrealized loss has no tax effect until a taxable disposition occurs. Begin with year-to-date realized short-term gains, short-term losses, long-term gains, and long-term losses across every taxable account.

Then add any capital-loss carryforward from the prior return. A dashboard that shows only current market losses can miss gains at another broker, distributions from a fund, or a carryforward that already offsets the planned sale.

Tax-advantaged retirement accounts generally do not produce a current capital loss deduction. Mixing them into the same harvesting worksheet can create a misleading total and can contribute to wash-sale problems when a replacement is purchased there.

## Understand the netting sequence

Short-term items are netted with other short-term items, and long-term items are netted with other long-term items. If one category is a gain and the other is a loss, they then offset.

That ordering matters because net short-term gains generally use ordinary income rates, while net long-term gains may use the preferential brackets. A $10,000 short-term loss can be more valuable against a $10,000 short-term gain than a simple flat 15% example suggests, but the final result depends on the complete Schedule D.

If total losses exceed total gains, an individual may be able to deduct a limited amount against ordinary income and carry the remainder forward. Gain Tax Calculator does not calculate the annual deduction or maintain carryforwards.

## Model a preliminary scenario

The scenario planner includes a hypothetical loss-harvesting field. Enter a real expected gain in the baseline and the proposed loss in the comparison.

Suppose a long-term gain is $85,000 and a documented long-term loss is $25,000. The preliminary net gain becomes $60,000 before other transactions. At a 15% federal long-term rate, the isolated federal difference would be $3,750. A 5% state assumption would add $1,250.

Those figures are arithmetic examples, not a promised saving. Other income can push part of the gain into a different bracket, another loss can change the netting category, and the selected state rate is not a complete state return.

## Treat the wash-sale window as a transaction system

The wash-sale rule can defer a loss when substantially identical stock or securities are acquired during the 61-day window that begins 30 days before and ends 30 days after the loss sale.

Review purchases in every account the taxpayer controls, including automatic dividend reinvestment and recurring investment plans. Replacement shares purchased before the sale can matter just as much as a repurchase afterward.

Do not assume that buying a different ticker is automatically safe or that two funds tracking similar markets are automatically substantially identical. The conclusion depends on the instruments and facts. Record why a replacement was selected and seek advice when the boundary is unclear.

Digital-asset wash-sale treatment can change with legislation and with the asset or product used. Confirm current law rather than copying a stock workflow to crypto.

## Preserve the investment plan

Tax savings should not turn a deliberate portfolio into an accidental market-timing trade. Before selling, identify an acceptable replacement exposure and write down the risk difference.

Check tracking index, duration, credit quality, sector and country weights, fees, liquidity, spreads, and tax characteristics. A replacement with different risk can underperform by more than the expected tax benefit.

Also consider transaction costs and the possibility that the replacement gain will eventually be taxable. Harvesting often changes the timing of tax and the basis of the portfolio; it does not always eliminate tax permanently.

## Keep an audit-ready record

For each harvested lot, retain:

- Account and asset identifier.
- Acquisition and sale dates.
- Quantity, proceeds, basis, and fees.
- Holding-period classification.
- Replacement asset and purchase date.
- Reinvestment or automated-plan activity during the wash-sale window.
- The return or schedule where a deferred loss and new basis are reported.

Broker forms can require reconciliation, especially when activity spans firms or wallets. Keep the source records rather than relying only on a planning screenshot.

## Year-end execution checklist

1. Reconcile realized gains, losses, and carryforwards.
2. Rank candidate losses by tax category and portfolio importance.
3. Review the full wash-sale window before placing an order.
4. Select a replacement that preserves the intended risk without assuming it is legally safe.
5. Recalculate after trades settle and after year-end distributions post.
6. Confirm state, NIIT, carryforward, and estimated-payment effects separately.
7. Review material transactions with a qualified professional.

The planner can show how one hypothetical loss changes a 2026 estimate. The tax return still needs the complete transaction history and the applicable netting, wash-sale, and carryforward rules.
